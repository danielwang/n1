# N1 design principles

**Evidence:** The themes below reflect prior N1 design discussions. They are **context-based working formulations**, not a verbatim or complete approved principles charter. Confirm wording and priority with the current product design team.

## Product and experience principles
1. **Feel familiar.** Use predictable patterns and established conventions unless a new pattern solves a demonstrated problem.
2. **Keep people in control.** Make actions reversible where feasible; provide stop, cancel, edit and undo for consequential actions.
3. **Make AI understandable.** Clarify who is acting, what information AI uses, and what will happen next. Avoid unexplained automation.
4. **Make the next action clear.** Use appropriate progressive disclosure, familiar hierarchy and helpful defaults.
5. **Design around user jobs.** Match workflows to students, educators, admins and organizational needs rather than a feature inventory.
6. **Support trust and recovery.** Show limitations and uncertainty appropriately; communicate errors in actionable language.
7. **Respect context and device.** Chat and voice may be mobile-first, while dense administrative tasks can be desktop-focused.
8. **Be inclusive and accessible.** Ensure keyboard/screen-reader paths, understandable content, colour-independent state and reduced motion.
9. **Enable consistent customization.** Allow institutions to reflect their identity without fragmenting the core user experience.
10. **Learn from evidence.** Treat hypotheses as hypotheses and prefer research/analytics over unsupported assumptions.

## Visual effectiveness
- Prioritize scanability, hierarchy, legibility, consistency and clear affordances.
- Prefer restrained decoration that reinforces meaning; motion should communicate state or continuity.
- Adapt branding at an approved customization layer, not through ad hoc per-screen treatments.

## Decision checks
- Which user goal improves? What happens on first use and return use?
- Is the action discoverable and predictable? Can the user change their mind?
- Does the experience clearly separate AI output from confirmed system action?
- Are the same rules carried across chat, agent and admin surfaces?
- Is there evidence for the pattern, or is it an experiment needing validation?
